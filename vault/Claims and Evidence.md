# Claims and Evidence

A quantity can be well-defined without being recoverable from the available evidence. Truth, knowability in principle, practical observability, and a defensible estimate are different questions. [Quantifiable Human Facts](Quantifiable%20Human%20Facts.md) defines the kind of quantity Roughly seeks to investigate.

## Target, observation, inference

The target is what a claim is about: for example, the proportion of a defined population that has experienced an event by a cutoff time. An observation is what a source records: an answer, a registry entry, or a directly measured occurrence. Inference connects those observations to the target and requires assumptions whose limits should be visible.

“How many respondents said they had entered salt water?” is not identical to “how many humans have entered salt water?” Neither a complete response log nor precise arithmetic supplies the missing coverage or measurement validity.

A source can provide stronger evidence for a particular claim without being authoritative for every nearby claim. A vehicle registry may record registered color, not current paint color. A transaction may record a purchase, not the purchaser's preference. Source meaning, coverage, timeliness, and error belong with the claim.

## Beyond polling

Surveys, administrative records, observations, and other datasets can support different targets. The product ambition is not confined to collecting poll answers. A broader source model needs explicit provenance and interpretation; it is a direction to investigate rather than an implemented ingestion capability.

[Polling Methodology](Polling%20Methodology.md) addresses survey-specific limits. [Read Models](Read%20Models.md) explains why application records and derived counts have an internal provenance, while [Development Fixtures](Development%20Fixtures.md) supplies no empirical evidence at all.
