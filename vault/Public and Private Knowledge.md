# Public and Private Knowledge

The public repository needs enough shared context for an independent contributor to understand the product, question a decision, and make a change. Product reasoning, architecture, methods, proposals, work records, and significant decisions belong with the project when they serve that purpose.

Raw personal reflection, private company comparisons, credentials, sensitive response data, and unreviewed reference dumps belong outside the repository. Promote a useful finding or decision with appropriate evidence; the shared explanation must stand on its own without access to private scratch notes.

[Participant Identity](Participant%20Identity.md) treats respondent privacy as a separate product and storage concern; a public knowledge graph must not expose private response histories.

Temporal content is not inherently private. A dated public research observation can belong in discovery, and a public decision record can explain why a choice changed. [Documentation Guidelines](_meta/Documentation%20Guidelines.md) distinguishes those forms from evergreen knowledge. [Project Workflow](Project%20Workflow.md) connects work and review without requiring private context.
