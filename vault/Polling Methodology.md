# Polling Methodology

A reported percentage describes a defined set of eligible observations. Generalizing it to a wider population requires a defensible sampling and estimation method. Response count alone does not establish representativeness, statistical significance, or a margin of error.

Wording, response options, collection mode, participation incentives, exclusions, and prior information all affect interpretation. Publish enough provenance to let readers understand the result: the question and options, sample source, collection period, inclusion rules, counts, and analysis method. [AAPOR best practices](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/best-practices/) and [disclosure standards](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/disclosure-standards/) provide research starting points.

[Results Exposure](Results%20Exposure.md) concerns the information shown before an answer. [Participant Identity](Participant%20Identity.md) separates privacy and abuse-control choices. Neither is resolved by adding an account system or a larger sample.

[Synthetic Preview Data](Synthetic%20Preview%20Data.md) describes examples that provide no evidence about respondents. An internal audit trail under [Domain Writes](Domain%20Writes.md) records application facts; it does not by itself make the collected answers truthful.
